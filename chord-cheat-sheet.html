Your 4-Chord Cheat Sheet

From the video: How Many Chords Do You Actually Need? · by Lara Bruyelles

You don't need a hundred chords to accompany yourself. You need four. These same four show up in more pop songs than you'd expect, because they all belong to one simple pattern.

The four chords

Play them in this order and your hand already knows the shape of dozens of songs.

C
C · E · G
Simple: C + G
Am
A · C · E
Simple: A + E
F
F · A · C
Simple: F + C
G
G · B · D
Simple: G + D
C → A minor → F → G → back to C
Too many notes? Play just the root and the fifth of each chord, the two outer notes in the "Simple" line above. It still sounds right, and it's an easier place to start.
Left hand. Add one note in your left hand, the root of the chord you're playing, an octave lower, C then A then F then G. That's it, that's your bass line.

Songs you can play right now

  • Let It Be · The BeatlesStraight C, Am, F, G, in order.
  • Stand By Me · Ben E. KingSame four chords, same order.
  • I'm Yours · Jason MrazSame four chords, with a syncopated left hand, see the patterns below.
  • Perfect · Ed SheeranSame four chords, reordered: C, G, A minor, F. Proof you can reorder these chords and get a different song.

Three ways to play them

1. Hold it
Just hold the chord down while you sing. Start here. It's not boring, it's clear, and your voice is what people are listening to anyway.
2. Play it twice
Play the chord, then play it again, right hand only. A small amount of movement that already sounds more alive.
3. Root, fifth, root (the one Lara used on cruise ships)
Left hand plays the root, then the fifth, then the root again on top, with a short rest before the next chord. This is the pattern under "I'm Yours." It takes more practice, so only move to it once pattern 1 feels easy.

What if a song has a fifth chord?

You will find one eventually. Here's what to do, in order of least to most effort.

  1. Skip the song. There are plenty of others that use only these four.
  2. Swap in a chord you already know. In "Flowers" by Miley Cyrus, the song uses a D minor. Play F instead, it's close enough and keeps you moving.
  3. Change the melody slightly so it fits over a chord you already know, rather than forcing a new one.
  4. Learn the one new chord. Sometimes it's genuinely simple, D minor is the exact same shape as C major, just one note higher.
